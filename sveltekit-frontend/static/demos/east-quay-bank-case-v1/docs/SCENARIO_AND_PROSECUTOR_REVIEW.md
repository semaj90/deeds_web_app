# FICTIONAL CASE — State v. Vale et al.

**Case:** `PA-DEMO-ROBBERY-001`  
**Classification:** `SYNTHETIC_TRAINING_NOT_EVIDENCE`  
**Date:** Wednesday, October 14, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles, illustrative)  
**Place:** North Quay Community Bank, East Quay District, Port Argent (all invented).  
**GPS:** unavailable. The scenario uses local scene coordinates in meters, not a claim about any real location.

## Who, what, why, how

**Who.** Rowan Vale, Cole Mercer, and Theo Knox are portrayed in the hypothetical narrative as three armed men who enter a bank with a fourth person, Mara Solis. The woman is not depicted as armed. Police later detain Cole and Mara in separate encounters outside. Rowan and Theo have not been located in the initial scenario. Elena Brooks is the bank teller, Imani Reed the branch manager, Yusuf Chen a customer, and Leon Park the responding officer. Every person, relationship, name, interview and portrait in this fixture is invented.

**What.** At approximately 3:04 p.m. the group enters the lobby and passes numerous people. The story estimates roughly eleven customers and workers in the public area, but that figure is not independently validated by its fictional source records. Shortly after entry, witnesses describe three men displaying what appear to be weapons. Several civilians are ordered toward the rear service corridor. A security alarm activates. Other people run toward public exits. A male voice demands that the teller give them all the money. The synthetic accounting worksheet later places the *possible* shortage at $86,240, not yet reconciled. Four figures are described leaving at different moments. Two persons are stopped outside and later interviewed; two are not apprehended during this interval.

**Why.** The prosecutor's starting *hypothesis* is financial gain. Whether each participant shared that motive or agreed beforehand remains unproven. For Mara especially, arriving with the group is not evidence that she intended or agreed to commit a robbery. A prosecutor must investigate those elements person by person.

**How.** According to the fictional witness accounts, intimidation, oral demands, movement restrictions and an ensuing alarm formed the confrontation. The exhibit descriptions don't establish who activated the alarm, the identity of every person seen in low-resolution camera views, the exact type of each weaponlike object, or whether any particular person moved funds. There are no detailed escape routes, genuine weapons, real surveillance files, or real site plans. Scene animation must not turn these unknowns into supposedly observed 3D behavior.

## Prosecutor's review memo: issues for investigation

**Preliminary issue statement:** The available *exercise materials* portray a coercive bank confrontation and two later stops. They do not establish the individualized knowledge, participation and identity of every named person beyond reasonable doubt, or even that all underlying source material exists. In the fictional narrative the men are armed, but the prosecutor's evidence file is intentionally incomplete.

**Potentially supporting facts:** The incident timeline contains convergent fictional witness statements about a demand and movement toward the rear. Entrance/exit synopses and alarm logs broadly support the sequence, subject to clock differences and authentication. A provisional cash figure exists.

**Potentially exculpatory or contradicting facts:** Cole denies carrying a weapon and denies handling a bag; a camera description cannot independently identify him near the counter. Mara denies any advance knowledge or assistance; teller and manager statements do not place a weapon or coercive act in her hands. Yusuf cannot identify the object he saw and did not hear the counter demand. Witnesses give variable counts. No original camera footage or bank ledger is in this fixture.

**Questions the prosecutor should resolve:** Is the original media available and authenticated? Do timestamps match across the alarm, door, and cameras? Which speaker made the demand, and how reliable is identification? What amount was actually missing? Were the recorded interviews lawful and complete? Is there evidence of an agreement or purposeful assistance by Mara? Have all alternative accounts and favorable evidence been preserved and disclosed? What legal elements actually apply in the chosen jurisdiction?

**Disposition in demo:** `OPEN_REVIEW` / no charging or guilt decision automatically entered. Keep a separate case-level review state, person-specific theories, exhibit-specific authenticity states, and event-scoped 3D animation approvals.

## POI portrait-to-head rule

A person ID references a `portraitAsset` (SVG in this demo), `characterAsset` (low-poly geometry recipe), `headAnchor` (`joint.head`), and the actual mesh part `face.head`. Presentation can be a head-attached label/sprite or a projected canvas atlas. Neither is a face-recognition step. In production, do not apply a real witness/suspect photo without an authorized evidence link, source provenance, and reviewer approval. Preserve original photos as immutable assets.

The procedural head already contains UV attributes. The front-facing part of the head maps near U≈0.25 in the v2 generator; `src/poi-portrait-three.mjs` composites the head illustration in that region and uses `THREE.SRGBColorSpace` for color-map pixels. The face texture changes only the *display* and cannot relabel an individual or add an evidentiary event.
